# Stage 3 — flight, art and sound notes

Stage 3 uses original procedural aircraft, cockpit, transport, landing-craft, vehicle, flak, supply and coastline models in `lib/air/art.ts`. No external aircraft model or new downloaded asset is required. The mesh factories share geometries/materials and use instancing for coastal rocks, vegetation and obstacles. Damage smoke follows the last struck position as a target moves. Wrecks sink; their colliders stop blocking later projectiles.

The coast reuses the project's locally bundled CC0 sand textures, documented in [terrain provenance](pillbox-visual-assets.md). Daylight, reflective ocean and persistent water-impact visuals reuse the naval rendering modules through narrow interfaces. Ambient coastal haze is deliberately light enough to preserve the required target window. Portrait mode hides the decorative instrument panel; functional player instruments remain visible.

Audio reuses `pillbox-machine-gun-fire.mp3`, the modified **Light Machine Gun** by **KuraiWolf**, licensed **CC BY 4.0**. [Source and modification details](pillbox-audio-assets.md). The briefing credits the author. The impact, splash and aircraft damage samples are CC0 **Battle at sea** by **Eike Germann (Thimras)**; [source and conversion details](audio-assets.md). The piston/propeller bed, airflow noise and rack-release clunk are generated by this implementation. The mixer caps transient voices at 12, uses one gesture-started context, and fades/stops combat sounds on pause or exit.

## Arcade physics contract

- World units are meters. Forward travel is 65 m/s (about 126 knots). Each attack lasts 40 seconds; the approach is 6 seconds, turns 6 seconds each, and exit 8 seconds. Total: about 146 seconds.
- Bank/lateral travel and altitude follow damped controls in a bounded corridor. This is guided arcade flight, not a lift/drag or engine-power simulation. Turns are guided curves. The pilot camera exaggerates visibility; model scale, lateral boat drift and effects are tuned for playability.
- Guns are depressed toward a shared convergence point 500 m ahead and 155 m below. Each actual muzzle launches at 640 m/s plus aircraft velocity. The gun sight uses the same ballistic path; if it has no nearby surface crossing, it shows a point along that path.
- Six bombs alternate physical racks. Release snapshots aircraft velocity; later steering does not move an airborne bomb. Gravity is 9.81 m/s², without drag or wind. Prediction and collision share that path. Targets use swept box colliders; blasts use horizontal distance to those bounds, with a 54 m outer radius. Visual detail is finer than the colliders.
- Flak fires at a fixed predicted position, with a 2.35-second fuse and 6.8-second battery cadence. The first shot is no earlier than 8 seconds into the mission. Damage is limited to a 25 m burst radius, up to 10 integrity points. Destroying a battery stops new shots but does not erase shells already airborne.
- Projectiles, target health, scoring and mission clocks run at fixed 60 Hz. The renderer, reticles and effects cannot alter a hit. Bombs have a 12-second lifetime, and the final result waits for committed bombs.

See [validation](stage-3-validation.md) for measured results and remaining platform checks.
